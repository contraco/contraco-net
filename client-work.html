Case 1, travel distribution

A leading European tour operator

Client
The executive responsible for the controlled distribution of a leading European tour operator, meaning its own travel agencies together with its franchise and cooperation partners.
The question
How does a store channel that carries a large share of the profit stay relevant when travelers start to plan and book through AI assistants?
What contraco did
Strategic positioning of the channel, IT advisory and executive presentations. The work treats the hotel knowledge held by travel advisers as the asset an AI assistant recommends from, makes the reliability of branch technology measurable before new money is spent, and ties every argument to the channel's own performance indicators.
Where it stands
Ongoing since June 2026.
Case 2, payments

A European payment service provider

Client
A European payment service provider preparing for purchases that AI agents trigger on a customer's behalf.
The question
Card networks and token schemes recognize an agent and secure the payment. Who checks whether this agent is allowed to trigger this transaction, under which conditions, and with a record that holds up in a dispute?
What contraco did
Project and product management across a program in three phases, being design, proof of concept and launch, and co-development of a risk score for agents. The score decides which payment methods an agent may use, and above a threshold that depends on risk and amount it brings a person back into the decision. The client owns the result.
Where it stands
Ongoing since June 2026.
Case 3, logistics and capital markets

A Southeast Asian logistics company

Client
The founder of a Southeast Asian logistics company, introduced through a regional advisory firm.
The question
Would a listing on a German exchange lift the valuation of the company and give the founder liquidity?
What contraco did
We scored three routes, the open market, the EU-regulated market and a strategic sale, against the four goals the founder named, which are prestige, a higher valuation, liquidity and a clean exit. Only a strategic sale to a European buyer met all four. A listing would have kept the founder in place as the operating principal, which is the outcome he had already rejected in an earlier offer for a majority stake.
Where it stands
Ongoing since July 2026.
Case 4, online travel

A European online travel group

Client
The head of a business unit at an online travel group active in more than a dozen markets, who met contraco at an industry congress and asked how we could help.
The question
How does an online travel company stay findable and trusted when AI agents do the searching? Supplier content reads the same everywhere, agent queries drive up the cost of searching against booking on tour operator inventory, and the checkout was built for people.
What contraco did
The work rests on three levers, being content that agents recommend, agent traffic that stays affordable and bookable, and clear ownership of the customer when an agent books. The work is designed as one project for the group, tested first on package holidays in one region.
Where it stands
Ongoing since October 2026.
Case 5, IT services

A German IT consultancy

Client
The managing directors of a German IT consultancy working in enterprise architecture, identity governance and integration.
The question
How does a firm whose demand rests on a few personal networks and on partner channels build demand that carries its own name?
What contraco did
Research with former clients and contacts, a pilot in each business unit that sells a fixed-price architecture or governance audit into existing clients, and an expert program in which the firm's specialists publish on LinkedIn every week.
Where it stands
Ongoing since December 2025.
Case 6, market entry

A US cloud ERP platform

Client
A US cloud ERP and platform company preparing its entry into Germany, Austria and Switzerland.
The question
Is the platform ready for German enterprise buyers, and what stands between it and a credible launch?
What contraco did
A market entry assessment. It rated the technical base at about 85 percent of market readiness, with a German interface, enterprise invoicing, value-added tax, multi-currency billing, open interfaces, a multi-tenant architecture and enterprise security. The gaps lay in accounting standards, where German buyers expect the standard charts of accounts SKR03 and SKR04, in customer success operations and in localized content. Without the accounting standards the platform could not claim market readiness.
Where it stands
Ongoing since November 2025. Why such gaps decide a launch in Germany is set out in our European Market Entry series.

What the six have in common

Each began with a question the client could not settle alone, and in none of them did the answer turn out to be technology alone. Three of the six concern AI agents that search, recommend, book or pay on someone's behalf. The other three concern how a company builds demand, finds the right route to capital, or prepares a launch in a new market.

We describe the engagements from our own records and leave out anything that would identify a client.
