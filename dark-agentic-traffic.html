Dark Agentic Traffic, or Why Your Reporting Hides the Shift

Nine of ten AI-driven visits show up in web analytics as branded search or direct. Saying we do not notice any of this proves an attribution gap, not the absence of change.

By Frank Meltke, contraco | August 2026

The Measurement Problem

Web analytics was designed to record how humans arrive at a site: they click a link, land on a page, and the referring source is captured. When an AI agent does the research and a human arrives later as a result of that research, the attribution chain is broken at the point the human acts on what the agent found. The click that analytics records is the human's click, not the agent's recommendation.

This is not a minor accounting issue. It means that the fastest-growing influence on commercial decisions in 2026 is systematically invisible to the reporting systems most organizations use to make their marketing and product decisions.

What the Data Shows

Similarweb's Downstream study maps what happens after an AI session ends. AI-influenced visitors arrive at destination sites at 2.5 times the rate of baseline within seven days of the AI session. When they arrive, they engage at roughly double the depth: 12.0 pages per session and 11.8 minutes on site, compared to 6.5 pages and 5.6 minutes for visitors without AI-session history. [Q14]

Only 8.8% of AI-influenced traffic arrives labeled as a direct AI referral. 55.9% arrives as branded search.

Source: Similarweb Downstream study (Q14)

The 55.9 percent branded search figure is the critical point. When someone asks an AI which product to buy, the AI names a brand, and the person later searches for that brand, analytics records a branded search. The AI recommendation that drove the branded search is invisible. [Q14]

The zero-click problem compounds this. Approximately 65 to 68 percent of Google searches now end without a click. The AI reads the answer from the search results page and returns it to the user without the source site ever receiving a visit. [Q14]

What Adobe Analytics Adds

Similarweb measures volume and behavior. Adobe Analytics adds a commercial dimension. Traffic arriving from AI sources converts at 42 percent above the baseline conversion rate. Revenue per visit is 37 percent higher. [Q11]

AI-referred traffic: 42% higher conversion rate, 37% higher revenue per visit.

Source: Adobe Analytics report (Q11)

The practical consequence: the minority of traffic that arrives with an AI attribution label is the highest-quality segment in the data. The majority, which arrives as branded search or direct, contains an invisible AI-influenced component that is pulling conversion rates and revenue per visit upward in ways that standard attribution cannot isolate.

Organizations that optimize based only on what they can see are optimizing for the less valuable minority of their traffic and misreading the signals from their best-performing segment.

Optimizing for Visibility Means Optimizing for the Minority

The practical management problem is this: every standard analytics report shows a world in which AI barely exists as a traffic source. Every optimization decision made from that report is therefore optimizing for a world that no longer reflects commercial reality.

The shift is not future. The Adobe data shows a swing within a twelve-month window from a negative AI traffic contribution to a plus-37-percent revenue-per-visit figure. [Q11] What looks like stability in analytics reporting is the result of an attribution gap, not the absence of change.
